# Phishing: Recognise And Respond

## Pause And Check

Treat unexpected or urgent requests for payment, login or information with care. Good spelling, familiar branding and a familiar display name do not establish who sent a message.

- Pause before following a link, scanning a QR code, opening an attachment or supplying information.
- Check the request through a known bookmark or contact details obtained independently. Do not reply to the suspect message or use its contact details to verify it.
- Report the request through your employer's known reporting channel.

This advice draws on the [UK National Cyber Security Centre's guidance on spotting scams](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/spot-scams).

## After A Click Or Disclosure

Stop interacting with the message or site. Promptly tell your workplace IT or security contact what happened, including what you opened or shared.

- If you entered a password, use trusted account-recovery and reset routes. Address other accounts where you reused that password, following workplace instructions for work accounts.
- If money or bank details were involved, contact your bank through a trusted channel.
- Preserve the message and other evidence. Do not install unfamiliar cleanup tools or investigate a work device yourself; follow your organisation's response instructions.

The [UK National Cyber Security Centre's advice after sharing sensitive information](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/what-to-do) covers account recovery, bank contact and notifying workplace IT.

> This is general safety guidance, not Romanian legal advice. Your employer's incident procedure takes precedence for work devices and accounts. Reporting routes differ by organisation and country.
