# Phishing In Numbers

Phishing uses deceptive requests to obtain information, money or access. Its scale can be measured in different ways. These three figures describe business exposure, reported phishing sites and reported financial harm. They are external context, not results from NIS2COMPASS. Sources were checked on 28 October 2026.

## 38% Of Surveyed UK Businesses Identified Phishing

In the UK government's 2025/2026 survey, 38% of businesses said they identified phishing in the 12 months before interview. Interviews took place during August-December 2025; this is not a calendar-2026 rate. The percentage is a weighted estimate across eligible UK businesses, not just businesses that reported an attack. The scope excludes sole traders and businesses without information-technology capacity or an online presence. See [section 4.2 of the official survey](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/cyber-security-breaches-survey-20252026/cyber-security-breaches-survey-20252026).

The measure includes attempts that businesses noticed and reported. It does not mean 38% lost money, were successfully breached or had an employee click. Unnoticed activity and survey uncertainty limit what it can tell us. It is not a Romanian or worldwide estimate.

## About 1.07 Million Reported Phishing-Site Attacks

APWG, the Anti-Phishing Working Group, recorded about 1.07 million reported phishing-site attacks during April-June 2026. Its international member and public reporting network supplies the observations. The unit is a reported phishing site, identified through distinct bases of web addresses, not an email, person or successful theft. See the statistical highlights in [APWG's second-quarter 2026 report](https://docs.apwg.org/reports/apwg_trends_report_q2_2026.pdf).

Reporting coverage affects the total; it is not a census of all phishing worldwide. The monthly sum differs from the printed quarterly total by one, so the rounded figure avoids false precision. It does not establish an annual rate or count unique victims.

## About US$216 Million In Reported Losses

The US Federal Bureau of Investigation's Internet Crime Complaint Center, IC3, recorded about US$216 million in reported losses in its 2025 phishing/spoofing complaint category. Spoofing means disguising an identity or origin to appear genuine. The report includes international complainants; this is not a US-residents-only estimate. See the loss table on page 8 of the [2025 IC3 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf).

This monetary total concerns complaints received during 2025, not necessarily events that all happened that year. It is neither an audited cost to society nor a count of distinct victims. Other categories, including business email compromise, are separate and must not be treated as interchangeable phishing losses.

## Read The Unit Before Comparing

A percentage of businesses, a count of sites and a sum of money answer different questions. They have different reporting systems and time windows. Dividing one by another would not produce a meaningful chance that you will be deceived. Adding them would not produce a global total.

None of these measures assesses the effect of this learning section or proves that NIS2COMPASS training has succeeded. For a local exercise, a meaningful comparison needs a documented population, counting method and equivalent conditions; the [metrics guide](../playbooks/awareness-and-phishing-metrics-guide.md) explains that separate task.

You do not need to estimate the scale of a scam before taking a safe next step. Practise with [Everyday Examples](everyday-examples.md), check requests through a trusted independent route, and use [Recognise And Respond](recognise-and-respond.md) if something has already happened.
