# Phishing Training

Phishing is the use of deceptive messages, calls or websites to trick people into sharing information, approving access or sending money. These voluntary self-assessments let you practise choosing the safest next step in fictional workplace situations.

The [Basic self-assessment](/docs/phishing-training/basic-self-assessment/) has the original ten practical questions, drawn from the project's practical knowledge check. They cover situations such as an unexpected sign-in page, an urgent supplier request and reporting a suspicious message.

The [Advanced self-assessment](/docs/phishing-training/advanced-self-assessment/) has ten additional fictional workplace scenarios that combine several convincing details, such as a familiar voice, a real email conversation or a genuine sign-in page. It is new practice grounded in public guidance; it does not amend or form part of the approved methodology annex.

Each answer comes with an explanation of the safest response and why the other options fall short. You can start with either level.

> **Before you start:** This is voluntary practice. There is no overall score, pass mark or certificate, and it is not a workplace assessment or evidence of formal training. You are not asked for your name or identity. Your answers stay only in the page's memory, are not submitted or saved, and are cleared when you reload the page or restart. The website host may keep standard access logs separately.

For background, see the [phishing awareness overview](/docs/phishing-awareness/) and the guide to [recognising and responding to phishing](/docs/phishing-awareness/recognise-and-respond/).
