# October 2026 Project Update

Public reporting boundary: 28 October 2026.

NIS2COMPASS has selected eight CYberSynchrony tools and prepared its awareness approach. This update explains their intended roles and the conditions that remain before activity-specific use and outcome reporting.

> This update covers tool selection and awareness preparation. They do not establish that every tool is deployed, that live pilot integration has succeeded, that training or campaigns have been delivered, or that improvement has been measured. No numeric awareness outcomes are published here. An outcome not evidenced in this update should not be read as proof that no activity occurred.

## Eight Tools Across Six Modules

The eight selected tools are:

- CYRESCUE: Cyber Security Posture Assessment (CSPA) and Purple Team Assessment (PTA).
- CYBERRA: Defensive Cyber Operations (DCO) response playbooks.
- CYBRITE: Anomaly Detector IDS, where IDS means intrusion detection system.
- CROSS-CORE: Blockchain Integrity Validator, for conditional synthetic-evidence evaluation.
- CYBERWISE: TrustSENSE, for a team-consensus assessment of artificial intelligence trustworthiness and readiness.
- CYBERGOPLUS: Attack Surface Assessment (ASA) and Policy Tools.

The [selected-tools guide](../cybersynchrony/selected-tools.md) explains each tool's purpose, expected output and limits. Six modules describe functional groupings; eight tools describe the chosen components. Wazuh and Suricata are supporting open-source components operated by NIS2COMPASS, outside the selected eight.

## Two Different Scopes

The two main pilots use a staged portfolio covering baseline assessment and evidence review, authorised assessment work, then later monitoring and evidence consolidation. This sequence describes intended use, not a completed deployment across both pilots.

Planned work for three additional organisations covers review of information they provide, practical priorities and awareness. This is the SME-focused light-touch workstream; SME means small or medium-sized enterprise. TrustSENSE, ASA and CSPA are relevant only where applicable and separately authorised.

> The light-touch workstream excludes intrusive testing and continuous monitoring. Participation alone does not establish that the project's SME-support target has been met. Project participation does not authorise technical collection, uploads or staff-facing simulations. Each requires explicit activity-specific approval, approved data handling and safeguards.

## Awareness Preparation

The planned awareness sequence is an initial simulation, role-appropriate training and a comparable follow-up, subject to activity-specific approval.

> This preparation does not complete the project's formal monitoring deliverable.

The [awareness approach](../phishing-awareness/project-approach.md) explains the safeguards and measurement conditions. Training attendance and completion remain separate from simulation delivery, clicking and reporting. Public findings must be reviewed at group level, with small groups suppressed where people could be identified. TrustSENSE remains separate from these measures.

For everyday advice, use [Phishing: recognise and respond](../phishing-awareness/recognise-and-respond.md).

## Conditions For The Next Stages

Use of DCO depends on an approved working connection for Wazuh alerts. The Anomaly Detector needs an approved working Suricata feed; the two inputs are not interchangeable. The Blockchain Integrity Validator remains conditional on confirmed interface and documentation, using made-up evidence only.

Assessment and monitoring outputs require human review. Any awareness comparison needs a documented counting method and comparable groups, observation periods and difficulty. Targets remain targets until the relevant evidence supports an outcome.

> This public update does not disclose participant identities, individual or organisation scores, weaknesses, raw evidence or testing instructions. It does not establish funder acceptance or NIS2 compliance.
