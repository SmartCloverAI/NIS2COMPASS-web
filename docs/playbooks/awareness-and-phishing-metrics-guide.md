# Awareness And Phishing Metrics Guide

Use this companion with the [Phishing Awareness lessons](../phishing-awareness/README.md) and [prepared learning method](../phishing-awareness/project-approach.md). Phishing is a deceptive request for money, information or access; a simulation is an authorised exercise using a fictional request. Training records and simulation events answer different questions.

> Position at 28 October 2026: these are planned measurements and expected records, not completed project outputs or achieved outcomes. The worked example below is fictional.

## Keep Records With A Clear Purpose

The expected record set includes a role map to connect examples to people's work, the teaching material, an attendance record, and knowledge checks where practical. Action-demonstration records support the completion decision. Initial and follow-up measurements document the comparison; an aggregate summary explains what the group results can support.

Count unique people who completed the assigned learning and demonstrated recognition, independent verification and reporting. Contact addresses, invitations and attendance do not verify completion. One person with two addresses is still one person; a shared mailbox does not identify a completed learner. Keep the completion count separate from successful message delivery, human clicking and reporting.

## Define The Counts Before Comparing

State the population, observation period, denominator and exclusions for each measure. Exclude test accounts and failed deliveries as appropriate. Validate human actions and filter automated security checks, which can open links without a person doing so. Repeated clicks by one recipient do not become several unique clickers.

The click rate is 100 multiplied by the number of unique recipients with a validated human click, divided by the number of unique successful deliveries. The denominator is successful delivery to recipients, not all attempted sends. With zero successful deliveries, there is no calculable rate.

The relative reduction is 100 multiplied by the initial rate minus the follow-up rate, divided by the initial rate. Calculate the difference first. A zero initial rate makes relative reduction undefined; report that limitation. Reporting is another measure and needs its own stated unit and counting rule.

## A Fictional Comparison

Suppose each of two equivalent exercises has 100 unique successful deliveries. The initial exercise records 20 unique recipients with a validated human click; the follow-up records 14. Their click rates are 20% and 14%.

The difference is six percentage points. Relative reduction is 100 multiplied by (20 minus 14), divided by 20: 30%. These are two ways of describing the same difference, not two improvements. None of these numbers is a NIS2COMPASS result or target.

Compare equivalent populations, observation periods, message difficulty and filtering methods. State relevant differences rather than hiding them in a percentage. Even a comparable before-and-after change does not prove that teaching alone caused it. TrustSENSE's group assessment of artificial intelligence trustworthiness and readiness is separate from these measures and from organisational maturity under NIS2, the European Union's cybersecurity directive.

## Authority And Privacy

> Each staff-facing simulation needs explicit activity-specific approval, agreed data handling and safeguards. Project membership alone grants no authority. Exercises must not collect passwords, authentication codes, documents or free-text secrets, or use harmful attachments. Individual profiling and staff rankings are excluded. Exercise results must not be used to score people for employment decisions. Technical collection and transfers need their own approvals.

Publish only reviewed group-level findings. Suppress small groups where someone could be identified, including through a combination of counts. Keep individual names, addresses, click records and disciplinary information out of public summaries. Review the records and comparison before claiming an outcome; a target remains a target until evidence establishes it.
