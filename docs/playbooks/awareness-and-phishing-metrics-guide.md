# Awareness And Phishing Metrics Guide

Awareness work helps people recognize and report risky behavior. Metrics should be aggregate and privacy-safe. For everyday advice, see [phishing awareness](../phishing-awareness/README.md); for the planned learning sequence, see [the NIS2COMPASS approach](../phishing-awareness/project-approach.md).

> Public reporting boundary: 28 October 2026. This guide describes planned awareness measurements, not completed training, delivered campaigns or measured improvement. No numeric awareness outcomes are reported here.

## Minimum Outputs

- Role map.
- Training material.
- Attendance record.
- Knowledge check, where practical.
- Baseline metric.
- Follow-up metric.
- Aggregate summary.

These are expected records, not a list of completed project outputs. Keep attendance and training completion separate from simulation delivery, clicking and reporting.

## Comparable Measurements

Document what is counted, the population and observation period, the denominator for each percentage, and the exclusions. Count unique human actions. Exclude failed deliveries from delivery-based denominators and automated security-scanning events from human-action counts under a documented method.

Compare equivalent populations, time windows and message difficulty. A change in these conditions can make a before-and-after comparison misleading. A zero initial click rate does not allow a meaningful relative reduction calculation.

TrustSENSE supports a group-consensus assessment of artificial intelligence trustworthiness and readiness. Its findings are separate from training completion, phishing outcomes and NIS2 maturity.

## Safeguards

> Staff-facing simulations require explicit activity-specific approval, approved data handling and safeguards. They must not collect passwords, authentication codes, document uploads or free-text secrets, use harmful attachments, or score people for employment decisions. Project participation alone does not authorise a simulation.

Training should be supportive and relevant to participants' work. Review records before drawing conclusions about any change.

## Public-Safe Reporting

> Publish only reviewed group-level findings. Suppress small groups where people could be identified. Do not publish individual names, emails, click records, staff rankings or disciplinary information.

Use aggregate counts and percentages only when the counting method and comparison support them. Targets remain targets until reviewed evidence establishes an outcome.
